the places

Every place gets the same bricks, so picking one more only puts one more brick on the field — the yard does not grow by what a place sent, only by how many places are standing in it.

the brick

The brick is 196 × 95 × 45 and never changes. Every place is given the same bricks × goods, filled from the top of what the filtering left, so a place that sent seventy goods and a place that sent four stand exactly the same height. Nothing is printed on the bed: the field is meant to be read from the side, and that is where the name goes.

the classes

The volumes class every trade under six heads, but only ever as a place's total — no single good is tagged with its class anywhere in the source. These are read off the names of the goods, which places about nine in ten of them. Turning a class off here changes which goods are the largest ones left, and so changes what the bricks carry.

the goods

—

Everything standing in the yard, biggest Werth first. Striking one out takes it from every table, not only this one — and on this page striking out a place's largest good does not shorten its brick, it promotes the next good up onto the face.

the faces

the press

the edge of a stroke wanders, the ink gains or starves, and the whole thing sits in grain — done to the pixels, so it is in the exported faces and not just on screen.

what is on which face

the long side in view, 196 × 45 — the goods, each run out on leader dots to its quantity and measure.
one end, 95 × 45 — the values of those same goods, flush right, on the same lines.
the other end, 95 × 45, or the back, 196 × 45 — the place's name, and nothing else, repeated down every brick it has.
the bed, 196 × 95 — bare.

With the Werth beside its quantity the end is emptied and the long side in view carries the whole reading — good, quantity, measure and value — so that with the name on the end in view nothing on the brick has to be walked round for at all. The name column pays for it: it gives up exactly what the Werth column takes.

Standing at the corner, two of the six faces are legible and the other four have to be walked round for. With the name on the end in view the figures go round to the end that is not, which is the one arrangement where a place and what it chiefly sent can both be read without moving. Put the name on the far end or the back instead and the figures come back beside their goods, at the price of a field that names nothing until it is walked round.
