what the harbour table says

—

The arrivals from that place, printed in a different table from the goods — ships, their Lasten, their crews, and the value the harbour master put on what they carried. A place with no line here is not a place with no ships: it is a place the table did not break out.

the top — —

The one face that is not a column of figures. The column heads were tried here — its front edge is the top of the stretcher and its right edge the top of the header — and they read as captions at a corner rather than as heads; they are a first line on their own faces now. So were the sum and the ships: a Werth belongs with the figures on the working faces rather than over them, and the harbour table is a different table on a different footing. What is left is what belongs to the whole stack and to nothing narrower — whose trade it is, and off which printing.
