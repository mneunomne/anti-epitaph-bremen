the stack — —

the brick is 196 × 95 × 45 and never changes. Three goods fit across a stretcher, so a stack stands as high as the place had goods to send — that is the whole of what the height means. With print every face off only the beds are drawn, which is all the plan shows and a great deal less to hold.

the field

the classes

The volumes class every trade under six heads, but only ever as a place's total — no single good is tagged with its class anywhere in the source. These are read off the names of the goods, which places about nine in ten of them. The rest fall to nicht zugeordnet, shown by default so nothing quietly vanishes from a stack.

the goods

—

Everything standing in the yard, biggest Werth first. Striking one out takes it from every table, not only this one — the long tail is what makes a stack unwieldy, and a floor cuts it without having to choose by hand. A good the page never valued is not small, only unknown, so a dash is never cut by the floor.

the top of the stack

The one face that is not a column of figures, so it carries what belongs to the whole stack and to nothing narrower: whose trade it is, and off which printing it was read. The sum and the ships were tried on it and taken off — a Werth belongs with the figures on the working faces, not over them. Worked out on der Deckel →, which sets this same face on its own.

the faces as files

—

One png a printed side, at the size the brick actually is: a stretcher is 196 × 45 mm, a header 95 × 45, the bed 196 × 95. The top comes off with the course that carries it — one to a stack, since every other bed in a yard is under a brick — and it is set even where the yard is standing with a bare one. The place's name is one drawing hung on every course, so it comes off once and not once a course; a face with nothing on it is not written at all.

They arrive as separate downloads — a browser will ask once whether it may save several files.

what is on which face

the stretcher, 196 × 45 — the good, run out on leader dots to its quantity and measure.
the header, 95 × 45 — the value of those same three goods, flush right, on the same three lines.
the bed, 196 × 95 — only the topmost course carries it: the place, which printing it was read off, and the sum.
the far end, 95 × 45, and the back, 196 × 45 — bare unless the place's name is put on them, and then it repeats down every course of the stack, the way a title repeats down the spines of a run of volumes.

With the Werth beside its quantity the header is emptied and the stretcher carries the whole reading — good, quantity, measure and value on the one face, which is the only way a stack says what a good was worth to anyone standing still. The name column pays for it: it gives up exactly what the Werth column takes, and a long name is clipped sooner. Emptied, the header is free for the place's name, even with both sides printed.

Printed on both sides those two bare faces carry the next three goods and their values instead, so a brick holds six and the stack comes out half as high. Nothing is lost but the standing still: no one in front of it sees more than half of what the place sent, and the name has nowhere left to go.
