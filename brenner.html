the bed

What the head can actually reach, not what the machine is sold as — a bed figure is always a little optimistic. The frame is built inside it and stands in the machine's own corner, so a job never asks for travel that is not there. The foot says the rectangle the head will keep to.

—

Bricks go into the frame in order — up the first column from the corner the head starts in, then up the next — and a cell holds a brick, not a face. It reads the wrong way round against a sketch on paper, and it is right for the machine: one brick on a sheet is one brick's worth of travel, not the length of the bed and back. So side B is burnt, every brick is turned over where it lies, and side D is the sheet after it, in the very same cells. Click a brick on the bed and then another to swap the two; the frame in order puts them back.

A brick shows one face at a time, and a face is only in focus at its own height: lying on its underside a brick's top stands 45 mm off the bed, stood on a long side 95, on an end 196. So a sheet is all tops, or all long sides, or all ends, and no two faces of one brick are on it. 410 will not take two 196s and a gap without either the margin coming off or a face going round, so the frame says which it did. The cells never move: every sheet of a side is the same grid, the last one only with its end empty, so a frame squared up once does for the whole run.

the burn

The file is written by the same emitter as the engraving desk — the dialect that has already run on the machine: G90 to the start of a line, G91 along it with S on every move, the whole sheet one raster so the head never stops mid-line. The rapid figure only changes the estimate: how fast G0 actually moves is the machine's own business.

the press

The same press der Stapel sets, and it comes over with the yard's own setting — the wandering edge and the ink gaining, which are the type itself. The mottling and the specks in the white are left behind: they are paper, and burnt they are a few hundred extra scan lines for a few hundred specks of char.

what is done

—

A face is registered as burnt when you mark it — by the sheet, or one at a time in the list. It is kept per volume and per place, so a stack half cut today is the other half tomorrow.
